Do Kalshi prices match what happened?

An actual-versus-expected study of 24,903 settled NFL and NBA player-prop contracts from 125 games, 5 October to 19 November 2026. By Harrison Hong.

  1. No measurable gap between prices and outcomes. Every price band paid out within -1.5 to +0.8 points of what its price implied, and no band's 95% range excludes zero. The data cannot rule out a gap of up to 3.1 points in a band.
  2. The loss was mostly the cost of trading. Buying YES on every contract at the quoted ask would have returned -8.8% (95% range -11.7% to -5.6%). The half-spread and the fee, both known before buying, were 80% of that loss. This is a hypothetical return, not a trading record.
  3. It held when any one game was dropped. Leaving out each game in turn keeps the return between -9.5% to -8.3%.
  4. Sportsbooks priced NFL overs too high. Across 2,375 NFL props, the over's hit rate was -3.4 points off the books' de-vigged chance (95% range -6.0 to -0.7), led by rushing yards (-8.3).
  5. The app's Normal curve was too thin in the NFL's right tail. On yards lines 2+ swings above the middle, 5.6% paid out; the Normal curve said 1.0%, a Log-Normal curve 6.9% and Kalshi 5.2%.
  6. One buying rule held up after the fact. NFL unders near the line at an edge over 3% returned +13.9% (+3.1% to +23.9%) on 376 buys, but the rule was found in this data. NBA overs (-15.2%) and NBA longshots (-38.4%) lost.

What this does not show

  • The sample is under seven weeks of one season. Only the bands from 20c up reach full credibility.
  • The games are the ones my app scanned. They are not a random sample of Kalshi.
  • Returns are at prices on screen with an assumed order size. They are not trades, and real fills could be worse.
  • Single-league, single-stat and single-rule results are exploratory. With this many comparisons, some ranges exclude zero by chance.
  • The buying rules were found in this data; only the paper trials are out of sample. Nothing here is betting advice.

Method, robustness checks, code and tests are on GitHub. There is also a one-page summary (PDF).
